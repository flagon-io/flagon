Growing here means taking on bigger, harder, more ambiguous work over time, and getting better at it. That's the whole model. We don't have a promotion committee, a points system, or a calendar that decides when you're allowed to level up. You grow by doing more, and the framework catches up to you.

## Progression is scope, not tenure

Your [level](/handbook/compensation) describes the scope and impact of the work you're trusted to own, not how long you've been here or how senior you sound in a meeting. So progression is simple to describe and hard to fake: you start owning bigger, fuzzier problems and making the people around you better, and at some point everyone can see you're operating a level up. Then we adjust your level and your pay to match, proactively, without you having to build a case for it.

That cuts both ways. Time served earns nothing on its own, and a title isn't a trophy you're owed for sticking around. What moves you is the work getting bigger. It also means there's no ceiling waiting for a headcount slot to open: if the scope is there, the level follows.

## Management is a role, not a rung

The usual career ladder forces good builders into management to keep climbing, losing a great engineer to gain a mediocre manager. We don't. Management here is a different kind of work someone takes on because the team needs it, not a prize for being good at building. You can grow all the way up by getting deeper and broader at the craft and never manage a soul. See [how we manage](/handbook/how-we-manage) for what that role actually is.

## How the growth actually happens

Nobody grows from a performance-review form. It happens in the work, so we try to make the work the teacher.

- **Real ownership, early.** The fastest way to grow is to own something that matters and be trusted to figure it out. We hand out problems, not tickets, on purpose.
- **Straight feedback, continuously.** You'll hear where you stand from the people you work with and in your [one-on-ones](/handbook/one-on-ones), not once a year in a formal review. See [giving and getting feedback](/handbook/feedback).
- **Room to learn on the clock.** Learning the thing the work needs is part of the work, not something you're expected to do only on your own time. Reach for the book, the course, the conference, the afternoon spent understanding a system properly.
- **Support to stretch.** When you take on something over your head, that's the point, and you'll have people around you to lean on. [Staying human at a distance](/handbook/staying-human) is part of how we make sure stretching doesn't tip into drowning.
