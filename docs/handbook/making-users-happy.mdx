We don't have a growth department, and we're not planning one. The plan is simpler and harder: make something people are genuinely happy to use, treat them well the whole way through, and let them tell someone. Knowing what "happy" actually means for real people isn't a guess, it's a habit of [listening to them](/handbook/listening-to-users) constantly. Happy users aren't a nice side effect of the business. They are the business, and most of what we do is downstream of taking that literally.

## Recommendation is the only marketing that scales

The most valuable thing that can happen to Flagon is someone using it, liking it, and telling a friend without being asked. It's cheaper than ads, more credible than anything we could say about ourselves, and it compounds. Every other channel is us talking; word of mouth is someone else vouching. So the question behind a lot of decisions is simply: does this make someone more likely to recommend us, or less? A dark pattern might bump a number this quarter and quietly cost us the recommendation forever, which is a terrible trade.

## We treat you the way we'd want to be treated

The whole thing runs on one rule: if a tactic would annoy us as a customer, it annoys you, so we don't use it. No manufactured urgency, no roach-motel cancellation, no support maze, no charging extra for the security that keeps you safe. If it only works because you weren't paying attention, it's out. This is the same instinct as [our values](/handbook/values), pointed straight at the person using the product.

## Support is part of making it, not cleanup after

When something goes wrong, that's the moment that decides how someone feels about us, and we refuse to hand it to a script. The people who build the thing help support it, answers are fast and honest, and "we broke it, here's the fix" beats a polished apology every time. The full stance is in [support and customer care](/handbook/support-and-care); the short version is that support is craft, not a cost center.

## Happy isn't the same as never annoyed

Making users happy doesn't mean saying yes to everything. Sometimes the kind thing is a straight "we're not building that, and here's why," or holding a decision that's right for most people when a loud few want otherwise. We'd rather be honest and occasionally disappoint you than agreeable and quietly useless. Happy, measured over years, comes from trusting us, not from us flattering you this week.
