Management at Flagon is a job, not a rank. It's a small, mostly-context-providing role that some people do because they're good at it and the team needs it, not a trophy you win for sticking around. This page is about that role itself. For how the teams are shaped, see [how we're structured](/handbook/how-were-structured).

## Management is not a promotion

In a lot of companies, the only way up is to stop doing the thing you're good at and start managing people who do it. We think that's a waste, and it quietly punishes great builders by turning them into mediocre managers.

So here, becoming a manager is a sideways move into a different kind of work, not a step up a ladder. It comes with more context and more responsibility for other people, not more status. Nobody outranks anybody. A manager is a teammate with a particular job, same as the person who owns the database or the docs.

<Callout type="brand" title="No empire building">
  Headcount under you is not a scoreboard. We're not impressed by a big team,
  and growing one for its own sake is a good way to make yourself less useful,
  not more.
</Callout>

## What a manager actually does

Mostly three things: unblock, give context, and grow people.

**Unblock.** When someone's stuck on something they can't move alone, a decision that needs making, a door that needs opening, a fire that needs a grown-up, the manager clears it. The measure of the job is how rarely people are stuck waiting.

**Give context.** A small company moves on shared understanding, not instructions. A manager's real product is context: why this matters, what we're actually trying to do, what changed. People who have the context make good calls on their own. People who don't need to be told what to do, and that doesn't scale and isn't fun for anyone.

**Grow people.** Notice what someone's good at and point them at more of it. Give the feedback nobody else will. Help them get better at the craft and get paid more for it. See [feedback](/handbook/feedback) for how we do the honest part of that.

Notice what's not on the list: assigning tasks. We hand people problems and room, not a queue. If you find yourself doling out tickets, something's gone wrong, either the person doesn't have enough context or you're managing too tightly.

## You can grow without managing

This is the important one. You do not have to manage anyone to grow in impact, seniority, or pay. The most senior person on a team might manage nobody at all. Depth of craft, the size of the problems you can own, the trust you've earned: those move your career and your compensation, with or without direct reports.

If you want to manage because you genuinely like growing people and holding context, good, we need you. If you're eyeing it only because it looks like the next rung, don't. The rung doesn't exist. Go be great at the thing instead.

## Managers are accountable too

A small management job is still a real one. Managers get feedback like everyone else, and pointed feedback at that, because a manager having a bad month affects more people than a builder having one. If your manager isn't giving you context or isn't unblocking you, that's a bug worth [raising](/handbook/feedback), and raising it is not a career risk here. Doing this in the open means the role can be questioned like anything else.

If you're weighing whether this kind of work fits you, [working here](/handbook/working-here) and our [values](/handbook/values) are the honest backdrop.
