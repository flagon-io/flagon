Every tool a company adopts is a small tax it pays forever: another login, another integration to keep working, another place the truth might live, another bill. A pile of half-used SaaS tools is how a company ends up with its own operations quietly rotting. So we're deliberate about what we add, and the default answer to "should we get a new tool for this?" is "probably not, let's see if what we have already does it."

## What we reach for

- **Fewer tools, used fully,** over many tools, used at ten percent. One thing everyone actually knows beats five nobody has learned.
- **Boring and proven** over new and exciting, the same instinct as [how we build](/handbook/how-we-build). A tool is infrastructure; novelty there is a cost you pay at the worst possible moment.
- **Open source where it's a real option.** We'd rather build on things we can read, fork, and self-host than rent something we can't see inside. It fits how we work, and it's rarely the wrong call.
- **Where the work already lives.** Most of what a company reaches for another tool to do, [GitHub](/handbook/working-in-github) and the handbook already do. Reach there first.

## Before you add one

Adding a tool is a decision the whole company inherits, so make it in the open:

1. **Say what problem it solves,** specifically. "It would be nice" isn't one. A real, recurring pain is.
2. **Check we don't already have it.** We probably pay for something adjacent. Duplicate tools are how one team's data drifts out of sync with another's.
3. **Think about the data.** Where does our information go, who can see it, and can we get it back out? A tool you can't export from is a trap with a monthly fee. Anything touching customer data gets a harder look, see [security](/handbook/security).
4. **Someone owns it.** A tool with no owner becomes a bill nobody remembers and an integration nobody maintains. If you add it, you own it until you hand it off.

<Callout type="note" title="Adding is easy, removing is the hard part">
  It takes a minute to sign up and years to notice nobody uses it. So we treat
  adding a tool as the bigger decision it actually is, and we cull the ones that
  stopped earning their place.
</Callout>
