Engineers can work anywhere, so the question we take seriously is why a good one would choose to build here. The answer can't be a foosball table or a mission statement. It has to be the actual daily experience of the work: what you get to do, how much of it is real building versus overhead, and whether the place lets you do the best work of your career. Here's what we've built for that, and what we protect.

## Autonomy that's real

You own decisions, not just tasks. We tell you the problem and the constraints, and then we get out of the way. Nobody here is going to sit over your shoulder specifying which function to write. If you need to be micromanaged you'll be miserable, and if you don't, you'll get to actually think. That trust is the default from day one, not something you earn by surviving a probation ritual.

## Ship to production fast

The distance between an idea and it being live for users is short here, and we work hard to keep it short. You can have code in production on your first day. That's a deliberate consequence of how we build, described in [how we build](/handbook/how-we-build): small changes, a trunk that stays releasable, and boring, reliable deploys. Fast shipping isn't recklessness. It's what you get when the path to production is clean enough that releasing feels routine.

## Minimal red tape

We keep process to the smallest amount that actually helps. There's no committee to petition before you can try something, no six approvals to change a config, no ticket about the ticket. The goal is that on any given day, the overwhelming majority of your time goes into the work itself.

## Good tools and a fast local setup

You should be able to clone the repo and be [running locally](/handbook/developing-locally) in minutes, not fight the environment for two days before you write a line. We invest in that: fast setup, fast tests, fast feedback loops. Slow tooling is a tax paid by every engineer every day, so we treat a sluggish build or a flaky local environment as a real bug worth fixing, not a fact of life to endure.

## Own what you build, end to end

You build it, you own it, from the first commit through it running in production. That means you get the whole arc: the design, the implementation, the deploy, and the satisfaction of watching real people use the thing you made. Ownership isn't a burden we hand off to someone else once the fun part is done. It's the point. The person who built it understands it best, and that understanding matters most exactly when it counts.

## Room for craft and taste

We care how the work is made, not just that it ships. There's room here to name a thing well, to delete the awkward abstraction, to make the code a pleasure for the next person to read. Taste is welcome. We don't mistake motion for progress, and we don't punish the extra thought that separates something that merely works from something that's genuinely good.

## Humane on-call, no hero culture

<Callout type="warn" title="We don't reward heroics">
  A 2am save after a preventable outage is not a highlight, it's a signal that something upstream failed. We fix the system, not lionize the firefighter.
</Callout>

On-call is real but it's humane. The load is shared, the pager is reasonable, and nobody is expected to be permanently reachable or to run on adrenaline. When something breaks, we handle it honestly and calmly: figure out what happened, fix it, and write down what we learned so it doesn't happen twice. Incidents are about the system that let it happen, not about finding someone to blame. Blameless is the only version that actually makes the software more reliable, because people tell the truth when they're not bracing for punishment.

## Time to do it right

Everything above depends on one thing: we don't run people at a permanent sprint. Sustainable pace isn't a perk, it's how you get good software over a long stretch instead of fast software followed by burnout and turnover. We'd rather ship a little less this week and still have a sharp, rested team next year. Doing it right takes time, and we give it.
