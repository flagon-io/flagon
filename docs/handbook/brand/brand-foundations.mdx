If [the overview](/handbook/brand-overview) is why brand matters, this is what it is made of. Three things: taste, a personality, and a clear idea of who is on the other end.

## Taste over polish

Polish is surface. Smooth gradients, on-trend layouts, a shadow on everything. It is easy to buy and easy to fake, and skeptical people read it as a tell.

Taste is the thing underneath: a real point of view, and the care to get the small stuff right because it is right, not because anyone asked. The exact wording of an empty state. Whether the spacing is even. What happens when the network drops. Most people will not consciously notice any of it. They feel all of it, and the sum reads as "someone made this on purpose." With generated slop piling up everywhere, that difference is what people are actually responding to.

What taste looks like here:

- **The details are the work, not the polish afterward.** Typography, alignment, the copy on a button, the error nobody plans to hit.
- **Everything on the page earns its place.** If an element has no reason to be there, it goes.
- **We go the extra inch for the person who notices.** More people notice than you would guess.
- **We enjoy it.** When the maker liked making the thing, it shows. When they were phoning it in, that shows too.

## The personality

Flagon should read a certain way, and consistently. A quick calibration:

| We aim for | Not | 
| --- | --- |
| Direct | Fluffy |
| Honest, including about limits | Corporate optimism |
| Plain-spoken | Jargon and fog |
| Dry wit, when it is actually funny | Try-hard jokes |
| Considered | Random |
| Warm | Saccharine |
| Confident | Arrogant |

The failure mode on the left column is trying too hard. The failure mode on the right is saying nothing to avoid being wrong. We would rather have a point and stand behind it.

## Who we are talking to

Mostly builders: engineers and product-minded people who ship things and have been burned by software marketing before. That shapes everything, because these are people who:

- distrust marketing by reflex, and can spot corporate spin in seconds,
- prefer "it does X" to "it empowers you to unlock X,"
- respond well to honesty, including honesty about tradeoffs and what we have not built yet,
- like wit and cannot stand forced humour,
- will read the source, because reading source is fun to them.

The right mental model is not an enterprise buyer or an executive. It is a smart, skeptical friend who builds things. Write to that person. Design for that person. If something would embarrass you in front of them, it is not done.

## Before it ships

Before anything with our name on it ships, run it through one question: **how would this land with a skeptical builder who has seen every trick?** If it would get quietly closed as try-hard or dishonest, change it. If it would earn a nod, ship it. That instinct, applied a thousand small times, is the brand.
