---
title: Require two-factor authentication
description: Turn on the organization-wide two-factor requirement, and understand what happens to members who haven't set it up.
---

**Two-factor authentication (2FA)** adds a second step at sign-in, a 6-digit code from an
authenticator app, so a stolen password alone can't open an account. Each person turns on 2FA
for their own account. An organization can then **require** it: members without 2FA can't use
the organization, from the dashboard, the API, or the assistant, until they enable it.

By the end of this guide your organization will require 2FA, and you will know what members
without it experience and how to check who is affected.

<Callout type="info" title="Before you start">

- **Role:** organization **owner** or **admin**.
- **Your own 2FA:** enabled on your account first. Flagon won't let you turn the requirement on
  otherwise (the dashboard disables the switch and the API answers `403`), so you can't lock
  yourself out.
- **API token scopes:** `read:org` to read the policy, `write:org` to change it.

</Callout>

## Steps

<Steps>

<Step title="Enable 2FA on your own account">

Go to your personal **Settings > Password and authentication** (`/settings/security`), and
under **Two-factor authentication** enable **Authenticator app (TOTP)**. Confirm your password,
scan the QR code with an authenticator app, enter a code, and save your backup codes somewhere
safe.

</Step>

<Step title="Turn on the requirement">

<Tabs>

<Tab title="Dashboard">

1. Open organization **Settings > Authentication** (under **Security**).
2. Turn on **Require two-factor authentication**.

If the switch is disabled with a warning to enable 2FA on your own account first, finish the
previous step. Turning the requirement off is always allowed.

</Tab>

<Tab title="API">

The requirement is `enforce_two_factor` in the organization's security policy. `PUT` replaces
the whole policy, so read it first and send every field back:

```bash
curl https://api.flagon.io/orgs/acme/security \
  -H "Authorization: Bearer $FLAGON_TOKEN"
# {"enforce_two_factor": false, "require_sso": false, "base_permission": "read"}

curl -X PUT https://api.flagon.io/orgs/acme/security \
  -H "Authorization: Bearer $FLAGON_TOKEN" \
  -H "Content-Type: application/json" \
  -d '{"enforce_two_factor": true, "require_sso": false, "base_permission": "read"}'
```

The API enforces the lockout check itself: if your own account has no 2FA, turning the
requirement on returns `403` with
`enable two-factor authentication on your own account before requiring it for the organization`.
The check applies only when the requirement goes from off to on, and never to organization
access tokens.

</Tab>

<Tab title="Assistant">

> Require two-factor authentication for everyone in acme.

The assistant proposes **Confirm: Update security policy for "acme"** and changes only
`enforce_two_factor`.

</Tab>

</Tabs>

</Step>

<Step title="What members without 2FA see">

The next time a member without 2FA opens any page in the organization, Flagon redirects them to
a **Two-factor authentication required** page that names the organization. It offers:

- **Set up two-factor**, which opens their personal security settings (outside the organization,
  so the redirect can't loop);
- **I've enabled it - continue**, which takes them back into the organization.

They stay members, keep their roles, and can still use their personal settings and any other
organizations that don't require 2FA. Nothing is deleted; they just can't use this organization
until 2FA is on. Leaving the organization is still allowed.

</Step>

<Step title="Verify it worked">

- `GET /orgs/acme/security` returns `"enforce_two_factor": true`, and every organization object
  (for example in `GET /orgs`) carries the same flag.
- The audit log records `organization.security_changed` with the summary
  `enabled the two-factor authentication requirement`.
- Sign in as a test member without 2FA and open the organization: you land on the
  **Two-factor authentication required** page.

</Step>

</Steps>

## Where the requirement applies

The API enforces the requirement, not just the dashboard. It checks every operation under
`/orgs/<slug>/...` (except leaving the organization), every assistant and MCP tool call that
names the organization, and every assistant conversation in it. A member without 2FA gets
`403` with
`this organization requires two-factor authentication; enable it on your account to continue`,
whether they call from a dashboard session or with a **personal access token**. The dashboard
adds the redirect to the **Two-factor authentication required** page on top.

<Callout type="note" title="Organization access tokens are exempt">

An organization access token acts as a service identity that never signs in interactively, so
the 2FA requirement does not apply to it. Owners and admins control those tokens' lifetimes and
scopes; review and revoke them as part of your policy. See
[Automate with access tokens](/docs/guides/automate-with-access-tokens).

</Callout>

## Common failures

| Status | `detail` | What to do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `403` | `you don't have permission to do that` | Only owners and admins can read or change the policy. |
| `403` | `enable two-factor authentication on your own account before requiring it for the organization` | Turn on 2FA for your own account, then try again. |
| `403` | `this organization requires two-factor authentication; enable it on your account to continue` | The caller has no 2FA. Enable it in personal **Settings > Password and authentication**. |
| `422` | `validation failed` | The `PUT` body is missing a field. Send `enforce_two_factor`, `require_sso`, and `base_permission` together. |
