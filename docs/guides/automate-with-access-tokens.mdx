---
title: Automate with access tokens
description: Choose between a personal and an organization access token, pick scopes and an expiry, store the secret safely, and revoke it when you're done.
---

An **access token** is a long random secret that starts with `flagon_` and authenticates a
script, CI job, or MCP client to the Flagon API. You send it as a bearer token:
`Authorization: Bearer flagon_...`. Every token has two limits: the **role** of whoever it acts
as (a token can never do more than its principal), and optional **scopes** that narrow it
further to specific kinds of operations.

By the end of this guide you will have a token with only the access your automation needs, a
way to check what it can do, and a plan for rotating and revoking it.

<Callout type="info" title="Before you start">

- **Personal access token:** any signed-in user.
- **Organization access token:** organization **owner** or **admin**.
- **Tokens are managed in the dashboard only.** Creating, listing, and revoking tokens is not
  available through a token, the assistant, or MCP, so a leaked token can't mint more tokens.

</Callout>

## Personal or organization token?

| | Personal access token (PAT) | Organization access token (OAT) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Prefix | `flagon_pat_` | `flagon_oat_` |
| Acts as | You | A service principal that belongs to the organization, named after the token |
| Reaches | Every organization you belong to, with your role in each | Only its own organization |
| Role | Yours | Chosen at creation: `admin`, `member`, or `viewer` (read-only) |
| Created in | Personal **Settings > Personal access tokens** | Organization **Settings > API tokens** |
| When a person leaves | Stops being able to reach that organization | Unaffected; it isn't tied to a person |
| Audit log actor | You | The token's name |

Use an **organization token** for shared automation such as CI: it survives people changing
jobs, it can't reach other organizations, and its changes are attributed to the automation.
Use a **personal token** for your own scripts and your own MCP client.

## Scopes

A token with **Full access** is limited only by its role. Otherwise, pick scopes; a parent scope
includes its children.

| Group | Scopes, broadest first | Covers |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Account | `admin:user` > `user` > `read:user` | Your profile. |
| Organizations | `admin:org` > `write:org` > `read:org` | `read:org`: organizations, members, invitations, security policy, SSO providers, audit log. `write:org`: manage those. `admin:org`: create, leave, delete, restore organizations. |
| Projects | `admin:project` > `write:project` > `read:project` | `write:project`: create and edit projects. `admin:project`: delete and restore, and manage collaborators, team grants, and owners. |
| Teams | `admin:team` > `write:team` > `read:team` | `write:team`: create and edit teams and their members. `admin:team`: delete teams. |
| Notifications | `notifications` | Your notifications. |

See the [scopes reference](/docs/reference/scopes) for the operation-by-operation map.

## Steps

<Steps>

<Step title="Create the token">

**Personal token:** open personal **Settings > Personal access tokens** and select
**Generate new token**.

**Organization token:** open organization **Settings > API tokens** (under **Developer**) and
select **Generate new token**. The button appears only for owners and admins.

On the form:

1. **Token name**: something you'll recognize later, such as `github-actions-deploy`.
2. **Role** (organization tokens only): `Admin`, `Member`, or `Viewer`. The token can never
   exceed it.
3. **Expiration**: **7 days**, **30 days** (the default), **90 days**, **1 year**, **Custom...**
   (pick a date), or **No expiration**.
4. **Permissions**: check **Full access**, or check only the scopes you need.
5. Select **Generate token**.

</Step>

<Step title="Copy the secret once">

The next screen says **Token created. Copy it now.** and shows the full secret with a **Copy**
button. This is the only time it is shown: Flagon stores only a hash, and lists show just a
short prefix such as `flagon_pat_ab12cd`. Put it straight into a secret store, such as your CI
provider's encrypted secrets or a password manager. Never commit it.

</Step>

<Step title="Verify what it can do">

```bash
export FLAGON_TOKEN=flagon_pat_...
curl https://api.flagon.io/me -H "Authorization: Bearer $FLAGON_TOKEN"
```

`GET /me` (needs `read:user`) returns who the token acts as and its organizations. For an
organization token, that is the service principal and its one organization. Then call an
endpoint your automation needs, for example `GET /orgs/acme/projects` with `read:project`.

</Step>

<Step title="Rotate and revoke">

Each row in the token list shows its access, **Last used**, and **Expires**. To rotate, create
a new token, switch your automation to it, then revoke the old one: select **Revoke** on its
row and confirm with **Revoke token**. Revocation takes effect on the next request. Revoking
an organization token also removes its service principal from the organization.

An expired token is rejected the same way as a revoked one. Organization tokens also stop
working when their organization is deleted.

</Step>

</Steps>

## Common failures

| Status | `detail` | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `401` | `invalid or expired token` | The token is wrong, revoked, or expired, or its organization was deleted. |
| `403` | `this token is missing the scope required for this operation` | Add the scope (create a new token; scopes can't be edited). |
| `403` | `you don't have permission to do that` | The scope is there, but the token's role doesn't allow the action. |
| `403` | `this organization requires two-factor authentication; enable it on your account to continue` | A personal access token whose user has no 2FA, in an organization that requires it. Organization tokens are exempt. |
| `403` | `this organization requires single sign-on; sign in through the organization's identity provider at least once before using a personal access token with it` | The organization requires SSO and the token's user has never signed in through its provider. Owners and organization tokens are exempt. |
| `403` | `this operation requires a user; organization access tokens can't perform it` | Organization tokens can't create, leave, or restore organizations, list deleted organizations, or sync a profile. Use a personal token. |
| `404` | `organization not found` | The token's principal isn't a member of that organization. An organization token can only reach its own. |
| `422` | `select at least one scope, or choose full access` | Shown on the create form if you pick nothing. |
| `422` | `expiration must be in the future` | Pick a later custom date. |

Over MCP, the same problems come back as a JSON-RPC error `-32001` (`invalid or expired token`)
or `-32003` (missing scope).
