You have **one Flagon account**, and it can belong to any number of
[organizations](/docs/platform/organizations). Everything personal to you lives
under **Settings** at `app.flagon.io/settings`, the one area of Flagon that sits
outside any organization. Organization settings (members, org tokens, billing)
live under each org's own **Settings** instead.

Your account and sign-in credentials are owned by the web app's auth service
(BetterAuth). The app mirrors your public profile to the API so the rest of the
platform can reference you.

## Public profile

**Settings > Public profile** is what other people see: your name, avatar, bio,
pronouns, website, company, location, up to four social links, and an optional
public email. Anyone can read it at `GET /users/{username}`, which needs no
authentication and returns only these public fields. A deleted account's profile
is hidden.

## Account

**Settings > Account** holds the settings that identify you:

- **Username**: your handle across Flagon. You can sign in with it instead of
  your email, and others use it to add you to organizations, teams, and projects.
  Usernames are unique; changing yours can break existing links to your profile.
- **Delete account**: deactivates your account and signs you out everywhere. It
  is reversible (support can restore an account deleted by mistake). You cannot
  delete your account while you own an organization: first hand ownership to
  someone else and leave, or
  [delete the organization](/docs/platform/organizations#deleting-an-organization).

## Emails

**Settings > Emails** lists the addresses on your account. Add and verify more
addresses there, and choose your **primary** email, which is used for sign-in and
for notifications. Accepting an [invitation](/docs/platform/members) verifies the
invited address, since the link proves you control that mailbox.

## Password, two-factor, and sessions

- **Settings > Password and authentication** changes your password (which signs
  out your other sessions) and sets up
  [two-factor authentication](/docs/platform/two-factor). An organization can
  require 2FA of every member.
- **Settings > Sessions** lists the devices and browsers signed in to your
  account. Revoke any session you do not recognize, or sign out every session but
  the current one.

If an organization you belong to uses [single sign-on](/docs/platform/sso), that
org's identity is linked to this same account, so one sign-in reaches every
organization you belong to.

## Personal access tokens

**Settings > Personal access tokens** (under **Developer settings**) is where you
generate and revoke **personal access tokens** (`flagon_pat_...`) for the
[API](/docs/api/using-the-api) and [MCP](/docs/ai/mcp). A personal token acts as
you, across every organization you belong to, and carries the scopes and expiry
you choose. See [Access tokens](/docs/platform/access-tokens), which also covers
organization tokens.

## Organizations, notifications, and appearance

- **Settings > Organizations** lists the organizations you belong to with your
  role in each. From there you create a new organization, leave one, and restore
  one you deleted in the last 30 days (**Recently deleted**).
- **Settings > Notifications** is your personal
  [notification](/docs/platform/notifications) feed across every organization.
- **Settings > Appearance** sets your theme: light, dark, or matching your
  system.

## Over the API and the assistant

`GET /me` returns your account and the organizations you belong to (scope
`read:user`); the assistant and MCP answer the same question with the `whoami`
tool.

```bash
curl https://api.flagon.io/me \
  -H "Authorization: Bearer $FLAGON_TOKEN"
```

Profile, email, password, session, and token changes are made in **Settings**,
not through the assistant, and the API has no operations for emails, passwords,
sessions, or account deletion that a token can reach. That is deliberate: a
credential should never be able to take over the account behind it or mint more
credentials. (The API's `PUT /me/profile` exists so the app can mirror your
public profile to the API after you save it in **Settings**. Only the app can
call it; every access token gets `401 Unauthorized`, so a token can never change
your profile, your username, or your two-factor or SSO state.)
