---
title: Two-factor authentication
description: Add an authenticator-app second step to your sign-in, and require it for everyone in an organization.
---

**Two-factor authentication** (2FA) adds a second step at sign-in, so a leaked
password alone cannot open your account. Flagon's second factor is a time-based
one-time password (TOTP) from an authenticator app, with backup codes for when
you lose your device. You turn it on for your own account; an organization can
additionally require it of every member.

## Turn on 2FA for your account

Go to **Settings > Password and authentication** (`app.flagon.io/settings/security`)
and enable **Authenticator app (TOTP)**:

1. Confirm your password.
2. Scan the QR code with an authenticator app (1Password, Google Authenticator,
   Authy, and similar).
3. Save your **backup codes** somewhere safe. Each lets you sign in once if you
   lose your device.

From then on, signing in asks for a 6-digit code (or a backup code) after your
password. 2FA belongs to your account, so it applies in every organization you
belong to.

## Require 2FA for an organization

An organization owner or admin can require every member to have 2FA enabled.

<Tabs>

<Tab title="Dashboard">

Open the org's **Settings > Authentication** and turn on **Require two-factor
authentication**.

</Tab>

<Tab title="API">

`PUT /orgs/{slug}/security` replaces the whole policy, so send all three fields:

```bash
curl -X PUT https://api.flagon.io/orgs/acme/security \
  -H "Authorization: Bearer $FLAGON_TOKEN" \
  -H "Content-Type: application/json" \
  -d '{"enforce_two_factor": true, "require_sso": false, "base_permission": "read"}'
```

Read the current policy first with `GET /orgs/{slug}/security` so you do not
change the other fields by accident. Requires `write:org` and an owner or admin
role.

</Tab>

<Tab title="Assistant">

Ask: "Require two-factor authentication in acme." The assistant proposes
`set_org_security` with only `enforce_two_factor` changed; the tool leaves the
other fields as they are.

</Tab>

</Tabs>

What happens next:

- Members without 2FA, owners included, are sent to a "two-factor
  authentication required" page when they open the organization, and must set it
  up before they can continue.
- The API enforces the same rule. A member without 2FA gets `403 Forbidden`
  (`this organization requires two-factor authentication; enable it on your account to continue`) on every operation under `/orgs/{slug}` except leaving
  the organization, on assistant messages and confirmed actions, and on MCP tool
  calls that name the organization. This covers dashboard sessions and
  **personal access tokens** alike.
- **Organization access tokens** are exempt: their service principal never signs
  in, so 2FA does not apply to it.
- **Lockout safety**: the API refuses to turn the requirement on unless your own
  2FA is already enabled (`403 Forbidden`, `enable two-factor authentication on your own account before requiring it for the organization`), whether you use
  the dashboard, the API, or the assistant. Turning it off is always allowed.
- The change is recorded in the org's [audit log](/docs/platform/audit-log) as
  `organization.security_changed`.

<Callout type="tip" title="Before you turn it on">

Because the API enforces the requirement, scripts and MCP clients that use a
member's personal access token stop working in this organization until that
member enables 2FA. Move shared automation to an
[organization access token](/docs/platform/access-tokens) first.

</Callout>

This pairs with [single sign-on](/docs/platform/sso): you can require SSO, 2FA,
or both. See [Require two-factor authentication](/docs/guides/require-two-factor)
for a rollout walkthrough.
