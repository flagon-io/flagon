Almost all of Flagon reaches people as words: the site, docs, release notes, support replies, the handbook you are reading. Voice carries most of the brand. The chapter on [how we write](/handbook/how-we-write) covers the craft; this is the brand-level register, the sound we are all aiming at so it holds together no matter who typed it.

## The register

Write like a competent person explaining something to a peer they respect. Not a brand. Not a bot. Not a hype man. A person who knows the thing, has nothing to hide, and does not need to oversell.

- **Say the thing.** Lead with what is true and useful. Get to it in the first sentence.
- **Short words, short sentences.** If a plain word will do, use it. Cut the one you would not say out loud.
- **Specific beats grand.** "Answers in a day, usually less" beats "world-class support." Numbers and nouns over adjectives.
- **Admit the edges.** Say what something does not do, or what we have not finished. It reads as trust, not weakness, and it is true.
- **Dry over loud.** Wit lands when it is a little unexpected and genuinely funny. If you are reaching for a joke, drop it.

## Words we do not use

A short banned list keeps the fog out. Avoid:

- **Hype adjectives:** revolutionary, seamless, cutting-edge, world-class, next-generation, game-changing.
- **Filler verbs:** empower, unlock, leverage, supercharge, elevate, delight.
- **Fake urgency:** "act now," "limited time," "don't miss out."
- **Weasel hedges:** "industry-leading," "best-in-class," "trusted by thousands" with nobody named.

None of these carry information. They are the sound of a company talking to itself.

## A few house rules

- **Second person.** Talk to "you," not "users" or "customers," wherever it reads naturally.
- **Contractions are fine.** They are how people talk.
- **No em-dashes or en-dashes in prose.** A comma, colon, or period does the job. This is a house style, and it is deliberate.
- **We are here now.** Write about Flagon in the present, as a company that exists and does what it says. Never "coming soon," "one day," or "when it is real." If something is early, say what is true about it today.
- **Own mistakes plainly.** When we get something wrong, say so in normal words and say what we are doing about it. No corporate non-apology.

## The gut check

Read it back as if a sharp friend sent it to you. Does it sound like a person who knows what they are talking about, or like a company trying to sound like one? If it is the second, rewrite it until it is the first.
