Every source the chapters cite, in their Further reading or in their text, is listed here once, so you can find the original behind any claim without hunting. Start with the manual. Read the source notes and papers when you want the why behind a mechanism. Treat vendor pages as snapshots.

Each entry gives the title, the author or publisher, the year where one applies, and the chapters that cite it. A chapter's Further reading says what a source is good for; this page says where it's cited. Living documentation has no year, so the chapters say when they checked each vendor fact.

Chapters are cited by short name: preface (*Before you start*); basics (*Know the nouns first*); latency (*Where the time goes*); storage (*Everything is a page*); buffers (*The buffer cache decides what stays*); MVCC (*Updates are inserts*); HOT (*Keep your updates HOT*); WAL (*Write it down first*); ACID (*ACID, honestly*); vacuum (*Vacuum is not optional*); not MySQL (*Postgres is not MySQL*); types (*Types are a contract*); keys (*Pick your keys once*); relationships (*Constraints, chosen on purpose*); models (*You don't need another database (yet)*); extensions (*Extensions worth knowing*); B-tree (*The B-tree does the heavy lifting*); more indexes (*Beyond the B-tree*); EXPLAIN (*Read the plan, not the clock*); planner (*The planner is guessing*); plans (*The cheapest plan wins*); queries (*Queries that age well*); search (*Search without a search engine*); functions (*Code in the database*); concurrency (*Everyone waits in line*); migrations (*Change the schema, keep the site*); loading (*Load it fast*); connections (*Connections aren't free*); benchmarking (*Measure honestly*); configuration (*The settings that matter*); observability (*Watch it run*); backups (*An untested backup is a rumor*); security (*Trust no tenant*); managed (*Someone else runs the box*); providers (*Pick a provider you can leave*); playbook (*Fix the work, then the box*); scale (*Scale up before you scale out*); replication (*Copies of the truth*); partitioning (*Partition for the delete*); sharding (*Shard last*); limits (*Where Postgres loses*).
