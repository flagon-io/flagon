Almost everything about PostgreSQL performance comes down to one number: 8,192. That's the size of a page, the unit Postgres reads, writes, and caches. A fast query touches few pages. A slow one touches many, or waits on someone else who is. Once you can see the pages, most of the mystery goes away.

You've probably met the symptoms already. The dashboard that was instant at launch and crawls now. The migration that "does nothing" and took the site down anyway. The table that keeps growing faster than the data in it. And the standing offer to fix all of it with a bigger instance. This book is the alternative to that offer.

## Count the pages, then earn the bigger box

The book starts at the bytes on disk and works up: how rows are stored and changed, how indexes and the planner find them, how to change a schema without an outage, and how to run Postgres in production. Along the way it builds two habits. Before you add an index, raise a setting, or blame the ORM, ask how many pages the work touches. And before you buy a bigger database, or a second one, use up the cheap fixes and prove in numbers that you've outgrown them.

That includes knowing where Postgres stops. On the same two million events, two column stores kept the data in under a fifth of the bytes and answered about ten times faster than a tuned Postgres. The book shows how far one Postgres goes, and the signal that tells you it's time for something else.

It's written for application developers who use Postgres every day, often through an ORM. You need basic SQL, not internals.

## Every claim that matters comes with a check

Every query ran against PostgreSQL 18, on a sample schema you can load in a minute, and every claim that matters comes with a lab check you can rerun on your own machine. Each chapter is a short essay you can read on its own; deep dives hold the machinery and the full measurements for when you want them.

## Free, and written in the open

Read it here, chapter by chapter, or download the whole thing as a PDF. It's versioned in git like everything else we publish, so when Postgres changes, the book does too. If something's wrong or unclear, that's a bug: [open an issue or send a fix](https://github.com/flagon-io/flagon/issues).
