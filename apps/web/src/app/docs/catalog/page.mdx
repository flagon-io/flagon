# Catalog

The **Catalog** is the heart of Flagon. It's a live map of everything you run:
your projects, the teams that own them, the repositories behind them, and how they
relate to each other, all in one place, on one foundation.

Where [Feature Flags](/docs/feature-flags) is a product you use, the Catalog is
the fabric the whole platform hangs on: it's how a project, its owner, its code,
and the things it depends on stay connected instead of scattered across a dozen
tools.

## What's here today

The Catalog is live. For every project you can:

- **Describe it**: a name, a description, a [kind](#classifying-a-project)
  (service, library, datastore, and so on), a lifecycle stage, a criticality
  tier, tags, and a rendered README.
- **Own it**: hand a [team](/docs/platform/teams) ownership, and grant other
  teams access GitHub-repository style.
- **[Link its repository](/docs/catalog/repositories)**: paste a repo URL to
  attach it. A hosted Git integration is on the way; manual linking works today.
- **Group it by domain**: tag a project with the free-form business
  [domains](#domains) it belongs to, and filter the catalog by them.
- **Add links**: runbooks, dashboards, on-call, and docs, right on the project.
- **[Map relationships](/docs/catalog/relationships)**: record how projects relate
  (depends on, part of, related to) and see both directions of the edge.

## The idea

Internal developer platforms tend to become another silo: a wiki no one updates,
a spreadsheet of services, ownership that's true until someone changes teams. The
Catalog is meant to be the opposite: derived from the same primitives your
products already use, so it's correct because it's *live*, not because someone
remembered to update it.

- **Projects**: what you run, described once.
- **Ownership**: which [team](/docs/platform/teams) owns each thing, so a page or
  a rollout always has an owner.
- **Code & relationships**: the repository behind a project and how it connects to
  the rest of the catalog.

## Classifying a project

Every project can carry a **kind** (service, library, website, datastore, tool,
ML model, docs, or other), a **lifecycle** (planning through deprecated), and a
**tier** (1 critical through 4 low). These drive the catalog filters, so you can
answer "which Tier 1 services are still in beta" at a glance.

## Domains

**Domains** are free-form business areas a project belongs to, like `payments` or
`growth`. They're plain labels, not a managed hierarchy: add whichever ones fit,
and the catalog lets you group and filter by them. Keep them low-cardinality and
they double as a lightweight systems map.
