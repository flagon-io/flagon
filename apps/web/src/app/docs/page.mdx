# Introduction

Flagon is a **developer platform**. Instead of buying a separate vendor for every
capability, each with its own login, billing, and model of your world, Flagon puts
the products you'd otherwise buy or build on a single foundation: one
organization, one login, one set of environments and teams, one bill.

Start in minutes on the hosted service at [flagon.io](https://flagon.io), or
[run the whole platform yourself](/docs/self-hosting) on your own infrastructure.
It is the same software either way.

<Callout tone="tip" title="New here?">
The five-minute path is the [Quickstart](/docs/quickstart): create an
organization, add a project, and ship your first feature flag.
</Callout>

## The mental model

Two ideas explain how everything fits together.

- **Substrate** is the foundation every product shares: your organization and
  teams, the projects in your [catalog](/docs/catalog), the environments you
  deploy to, and the way you authenticate. You learn it once.
- **Products** are what you actually use, built on that substrate rather than
  reinventing it. [Feature Flags](/docs/feature-flags) is available today, with
  more arriving on the same foundation. Because a product doesn't carry its own
  accounts or environments, a concept you learn once carries across everything you
  add.

The [Catalog](/docs/catalog) is the center: a live map of the services you run,
who owns them, and the products attached to each.

## How Flagon is built

Three commitments shape the platform, and they show up throughout these docs.

- **Standards, not lock-in.** Where an open standard exists, Flagon speaks it.
  Flag evaluation is [OpenFeature](https://openfeature.dev) over its remote
  protocol, so you use standard SDKs and could point them elsewhere.
- **Usage, never seats.** Pricing follows what you use, not how many teammates you
  add, so growing your team never grows the bill.
- **Always self-hostable.** Flagon is source-available and runs entirely on your
  own infrastructure.
